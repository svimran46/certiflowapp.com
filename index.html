An expiry date is not coverage.

Most contractors track certificates in a spreadsheet. A spreadsheet knows when a policy ends. It can’t tell you whether the endorsement that actually protects you was ever issued.

Endorsements go unread

Additional insured status lives on CG 20 10 and CG 20 37, not on the first page of the certificate. Without the completed operations form, a defect claim after handover falls back on you, and your carrier bills unearned premium for every sub who worked without it.

    Questions contractors ask us.

    Anything else, write to support@certiflowapp.com and a compliance specialist will answer.

    Do subcontractors or their brokers need an account?

    No. When you add a subcontractor, CertiFlow sends a private upload link. The sub or their broker drops in the ACORD 25 and endorsement PDFs in about ten seconds, with no login and no password.

    Does CertiFlow read endorsements, or only expiration dates?

    Both. Spreadsheets and low-cost trackers record the dates on the certificate. CertiFlow also reads the attached policy pages and looks for the ISO forms your contract requires, including CG 20 10 (ongoing operations), CG 20 37 (completed operations) and CG 24 04 (waiver of subrogation).

    What happens when a policy is about to expire?

    CertiFlow writes to the subcontractor and the broker on the certificate 30 days and again 14 days before expiry. When the renewed certificate arrives, it is audited and filed automatically.

    How does this help at our annual insurance audit?

    Your carrier will ask for proof that every subcontractor carried the required limits and named you as additional insured. CertiFlow exports a complete, time-stamped binder of certificates and endorsements in one step, so you are not charged premium for subs who appear uninsured.
